# Editorial direction

## Purpose

This is Eugene Vlasov’s personal public home. It should be recognizable to friends, reassuring to parents and collaborators, and a credible doorway into School of Code Calgary. Eugene remains broader than the school.

The governing principle is: **Do not professionalize Eugene—curate Eugene.**

## Narrative

Organize the story by recurring themes rather than employment chronology:

- science, physics, pharmacy, and research;
- computers, programming, networks, and business systems;
- research programming and data;
- Arduino, Raspberry Pi, sensors, cameras, pumps, robots, and plants;
- teaching and the need to understand what one has made;
- experiments, books, and strange useful projects;
- School of Code Calgary as the present focus.

Named organizations appear only in the compact “Along the way” section. Each receives one concrete sentence. This is evidence, not a résumé or logo wall.

## Voice

- Direct, thoughtful, personal, and technically literate.
- Dry humour in small quantities.
- Specific objects and actions instead of claims of excellence.
- “Friend of robots,” “The program is a text,” and “the boat is allowed” are recurring personal notes, not slogans to repeat everywhere.
- Avoid generic marketing language, job-seeking language, skill inventories, investor language, and corporate biography.

## Visual system

The page resembles an editorial laboratory notebook: warm paper, near-black ink, deep blue, warm red-orange, restrained yellow, serif headings, sans-serif body text, and monospaced annotations. Crisp rules, asymmetric compositions, and generous space replace ornamental effects.

The existing cartoon remains visibly labelled as a surviving artifact. It must not be represented as a current professional portrait. New imagery should come from Eugene’s real life and work; no stock photography or identifiable student photographs.

## Boundaries for later passes

- Do not add a CV or employer chronology.
- Do not make School of Code consume Eugene’s entire identity.
- Do not add company logos merely to imply credibility.
- Do not publish student photographs without documented permission.
- Do not add analytics, tracking, or third-party runtime dependencies by default.
- Do not add claims, dates, metrics, or biographies until they pass the factual review.
